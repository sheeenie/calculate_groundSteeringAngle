### Code of conduct 

# This page has the group's formed code of conduct, including how we plan to collaborate, how we ensure knowledge transfer and our communication plan, amongst other things. 

### 1. How do we plan to collaborate?
- We will collaborate using Git, and more specifically, GitLab. 
- We will create specific branches for features, called feature branches after the group creates and divides tasks/issues among us. 
- We will conduct code reviews before pull/merge requests, and we will also be using the concept of pair-programming to some extent. 
- We will not “ butt in “ on others’ work, even if you would do it faster. Furthermore, if a member is stuck on a specific task/problem, ask for help early to speed up the process as much as possible. 
- For the created issues, we have acceptance criteria which will act as our definition of done (DoD), where an issue will be completed **only** after all the check marks are ticked. // **Addition made by LLM**

### 2. How do we ensure that everyone in our group stays informed about the individual contributions?
- We will be performing textly-stand-ups in our communication channel of choice ( Discord ). 
- We will be tracking our progress of assignments on a Trello kanban board, whilst specific issues/branches will be tracked on the GitLab kanban board. All the members will help each other to move the cards accordingly, so that the progress reflects the actual work. 
- We will have one meeting every Monday to plan the week ahead.
- We will have one meeting every Friday to review what we have done during the week.
- Monday and Friday meetings are held in person, however since one member cannot come to campus for a short time period, the Monday and Friday meetings that specific week will be held remotely. If a member cannot meet up in person for the scheduled meetings, they will instead be held remotely. 
- The group has decided to work both remotely and in person. Tasks assigned to individuals can be completed in whichever setting they prefer, on-site or remotely, unless the group collectively agrees to meet and work together, either in person or online.
- The group will create individual "tags" in the kanban board, to ensure that the group shares the same vision regarding who is doing what, when something is due and how swamped someone is with their work. // **Addition made by LLM**

### 3. How do we ensure knowledge transfer among our team members?
- The group has created dedicated text and voice channels to ease the organization of information. To build on this, the group will inform the group if something was difficult or unclear during the lectures/work process, that way the members will be able to share their experiences and knowledge. 
- To keep the development process organized, we will create separate wiki pages alongside the text channels in our communication server, ensuring a structured and accessible knowledge base within our remote repository. This will serve as a central hub for important information, best practices, and solutions to common issues, ensuring that knowledge remains easily accessible and regularly updated.
- We will foster an open feedback culture where questions, ideas, and accountability are encouraged. Encouraging constructive feedback will help create an environment where team members feel comfortable asking questions, sharing their ideas, and admitting mistakes. Additionally, mentoring and cross-functional collaboration will be promoted, allowing experienced members to support others who might be struggling and enabling knowledge exchange across different areas of expertise. This approach will enhance knowledge sharing, keeping the team aligned on progress, potential blockers, and overall project development.
- The group plan to form "debugging-sessions" where bottlenecks will be resolved quicker, since a member of the group may already have run into a similar problem before, enabling us to break down the problems together as a team. // **Addition made by LLM**

### 4. What is our usual communication plan?
- We communicate and inform the group when we make decisions which will change the product drastically. Further, we will communicate when we run into problems, update the kanban boards, or when I as a group member do not have anything to do, so that the group can assign a new task to the member. 
- We will be performing textly-stand-ups which enables us to have a shared understanding of everyone’s workflow/blockers. 
- If an emergency occur in the project (regarding the process or product), the team can use the @everyone ping in the discord server. However, this shall be used with care, as too many pings can disrupt workflow, concentration and may lead to slower response rates all together. // **Addition made by LLM**

### 5. How will we solve conflicts?
- To avoid conflicts, the group makes decisions together. In the event of a tie, an open discussion will be held where arguments from both sides are heard. If a decision still cannot be reached after reevaluation, a coin toss will be used to break the tie.
- If there is a conflict between two members, they will deal with it internally. Further, if the conflict can not be solved in a civilized manner, a third member should be involved. Finally, if the conflict is impossible to solve, the teacher will be contacted and the conflict will hopefully be solved. 
- To prevent potential conflicts, members will adopt a “think-before-you-write” mindset when communicating through text. Since written messages can sometimes come across as harsh or critical, everyone is encouraged to consider how their words might be interpreted. Adding an emoji or using kind, encouraging language can go a long way in maintaining a positive tone and keeping group morale high. We remind ourselves that we're all human and working toward the same goal. // **Addition made by LLM**

### 6. How do you plan to ensure responsible use of LLMs in your project and how do you transparently and traceably document the use of LLMs? 
- It is acceptable/encouraged to take suggestions from LLM’s if it was your own idea, syntax fix etc. Hence, if you ask the LLM to fix grammar, add the correct number of curly brackets or something in that style, there is no need to document nor inform about that. 
- It is not acceptable to straight copy pasting code/other artifact created by a LLM instead of doing the work yourself. If there is a code snippet, document or other artifact which you contributed with, you should be able to explain the result. In other words; your creation is YOUR OWN. 
- If you need help by consulting a LLM ( which is encouraged ), be transparent and document what was taken, from where, and why. 
- Instead of just copy pasting code, text or other artifacts, the group will have a great understanding of the code written and developed, whether if it was created by a member or by an LLM. Further, if code is taken from a LLM, a screenshot will be taken of what the prompt was, as well as on the code that was taken. All text artifacts shall be written by the members ( derive from our own ideas), and text can be refined by a LLM ( this should be documented and honest, to ensure that our work is our own, and we shall not take credit for something which is not our idea ).
- To ensure transparency regarding code contributions helped by a LLM, Git commits will be tagged with #issueNumber *commit message*, **LLM-asssited**. This ensures that changes made by a LLM can be back tracked if something were to break, or if other documentation needs to be done. // **Addition made by LLM**
